<h5>
    Sejong Yang - CIP Lab, CS, Yonsei University
</h5>

Many PhD students accumulate multiple papers during their doctoral studies, yet struggle to unify them into a coherent thesis narrative. This talk shares a practical account of how I prepared and structured my PhD defense by reframing seemingly disparate research projects into a single, consistent research claim.
Using my work on character talking head generation and generative vision models as a case study, I explain how I identified a unifying research question centered on data and compute efficiency, and how concepts such as multi-prior alignment emerged retrospectively from repeated experimental failures rather than from an initial grand plan.
Rather than focusing on individual model details, this presentation emphasizes thesis-level decision making: how to choose core chapters, how to reposition side projects, and how to align one’s research identity with the perspective of thesis committee members. The goal is to provide MLSys students with concrete strategies for turning fragmented research outputs into a defensible and coherent PhD thesis.
