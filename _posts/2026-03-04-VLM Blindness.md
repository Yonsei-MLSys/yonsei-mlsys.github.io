Vision-Language Models (VLMs) achieve strong performance on many multimodal benchmarks, yet they often fail at surprisingly simple visual tasks that are trivial for humans. In this work, we highlight this phenomenon as VLM blindness: despite their apparent reasoning ability, VLMs frequently struggle with basic visual perception problems such as counting objects, detecting intersections, recognizing overlaps, or tracing simple paths. Through controlled experiments using simplified visual settings, we show that these failures arise not because the models lack visual information, but because they struggle to correctly bind and translate visual features into coherent language outputs. By analyzing these limitations through the lens of visual feature binding and recent findings on symbolic processing in VLMs, this work aims to better understand why such failures occur and what they reveal about the fundamental mechanisms of current vision-language models.
