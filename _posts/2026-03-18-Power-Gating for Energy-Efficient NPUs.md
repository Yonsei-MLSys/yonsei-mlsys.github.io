<h5>
    Hyeonsu Shin - ASO Lab, CS, Yonsei University
</h5>

This presentation examines power-gating techniques for improving the energy efficiency of neural processing units (NPUs), focusing on the impact of hardware underutilization across key components such as systolic arrays, vector units, and memory subsystems. It introduces fundamental architectural concepts and analyzes two representative approaches: UPTPU, which exploits the predictable dataflow of systolic arrays for fine-grained power gating, and ReGate, which extends power management to the entire NPU through a hardware–software co-design framework. By comparing these designs, the presentation highlights how workload characteristics and architectural features influence power-gating opportunities, and discusses the trade-offs between implementation complexity and energy savings. The goal is to provide a structured understanding of how power gating can address static power inefficiencies and contribute to the design of scalable, energy-efficient AI accelerators.
