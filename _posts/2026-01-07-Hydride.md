<h5>
    Kunmo Jeong - CoreLab, EE, Yonsei University
</h5>

High-performance Domain-Specific Languages (DSLs) like Halide are essential for modern workloads in image processing and deep learning. However, compiler infrastructures struggle to keep pace with the rapid evolution of hardware architectures, which now feature diverse and complex vector instruction sets (ISAs) such as x86 AVX-512, Hexagon HVX, and ARM NEON. Extending compilers to support these new ISAs typically requires labor-intensive manual engineering of intermediate representations (IRs) and brittle pattern-matching rules.
This presentation introduces Hydride, a novel compiler framework that addresses these challenges through automation and program synthesis. Hydride automatically generates a language-independent and target-independent IR (AutoLLVM IR) by extracting formal semantics directly from hardware vendors' pseudocode specifications. Furthermore, it employs a synthesis-based instruction selector to map high-level DSL code to optimal target-specific instructions, eliminating the need for manual backend implementation. Our evaluation shows that Hydride achieves performance competitive with, or superior to, heavily hand-optimized production compilers across x86, Hexagon, and ARM architectures, while significantly reducing the engineering effort required to support new hardware targets.
